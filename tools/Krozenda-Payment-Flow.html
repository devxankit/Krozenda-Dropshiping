1.Executive summary

Krozenda collects money from customers online and distributes it to three kinds of recipient: independent marketplace sellers, dropshipping partners, and Krozenda itself for its own stock and its commission. On top of that, the platform must pay its own staff. This document explains, end to end, how each of those movements works.

The design rests on four decisions:

DecisionWhat it means in practice
Online payment onlyUPI, credit card, debit card, net banking and wallets. No Cash on Delivery. Every order is fully paid before it is processed, so there is no collection risk and no cash reconciliation.
Vendor money moves on Razorpay RouteThe customer's payment is split by Razorpay at the moment of capture. Krozenda's commission and each vendor's share are routed separately. Krozenda never pools and re-disburses vendor funds.
Vendor payout is automatic, not manualEach vendor's share is transferred on hold at order time and released automatically once the order is delivered and the return window closes. No one presses a button per order.
Payroll moves on RazorpayX PayoutsStaff salaries and platform-owned disbursements are paid from Krozenda's own account through the Payout API, on a schedule. This is Krozenda's own money, so this rail is appropriate here.
The short version. Vendor money never sits in Krozenda's bank account — Razorpay splits it at source and holds the vendor's share until the order is safely delivered. Krozenda's own money — commission earned, own-stock revenue — settles to Krozenda's account normally, and salaries are paid out of it automatically each cycle.

2.The two money rails

Razorpay sells several products. Two of them move money out to other people, and they are not interchangeable. Understanding the difference is the foundation of everything below.

Rail A — Razorpay Route

Used for: marketplace vendor settlement.

The customer pays once. Razorpay splits that single payment and routes each share to a separate linked account — one per vendor — before the money ever reaches Krozenda's bank account.

Krozenda is a marketplace facilitator here. The money passing to vendors is not Krozenda's money and never becomes Krozenda's money.

Rail B — RazorpayX Payouts

Used for: payroll and platform disbursements.

Krozenda holds a balance in its own RazorpayX current account and instructs a transfer to a named beneficiary by IMPS, NEFT, RTGS or UPI.

This is Krozenda paying out its own money — salaries, reimbursements, incentives — exactly as a business pays any supplier or employee.

2.1 Which rail for which purpose

Money movementRailWhy
Marketplace seller's share of a saleRouteThird-party money. Must be split at source.
Dropshipping partner's share of a saleRouteSame as above.
Krozenda's commissionRoute (retained)Retained in the split; settles to Krozenda's account on the normal gateway cycle.
Own Stock sale proceedsRoute (retained)Krozenda is the seller; the full amount is Krozenda's.
Customer refundRefunds APIReverses the original payment to the original instrument. Not a payout.
Staff salaryPayoutsKrozenda's own money to its own employees.
Employee reimbursementPayoutsSame.
Vendor incentive or marketing rebate paid by KrozendaPayoutsKrozenda's own money, paid as a business expense — distinct from the vendor's sale proceeds.
Goodwill compensation to a customer outside the refund windowPayoutsCannot be reversed through the gateway once the window has closed.
The line that must not be crossed. Vendor sale proceeds must never travel on Rail B. Collecting customers' payments into Krozenda's own account and then paying vendors out of it by Payout API is aggregation, and under the RBI Payment Aggregator framework that activity requires a Payment Aggregator licence, which Krozenda does not hold. It would also mean vendor money sitting in Krozenda's account, exposed to Krozenda's creditors. This is a licensing question, not an engineering preference — see section 3.

3.Why Route is mandatory — the RBI position

Under the RBI's Guidelines on Regulation of Payment Aggregators and Payment Gateways, an entity that collects funds from customers on behalf of merchants and then settles those funds to the merchants is acting as a Payment Aggregator, and must be authorised to do so. Funds so collected must sit in an escrow account with a scheduled commercial bank, not in the entity's own operating account.

Krozenda is not an authorised Payment Aggregator. The compliant path is therefore to use one that is — Razorpay — and to have Razorpay perform the split itself:

  • Razorpay holds the funds in its own regulated escrow.
  • Razorpay performs the split into linked accounts.
  • Razorpay settles each linked account to its own registered bank account on its own cycle.
  • Krozenda instructs the split, but never custodies the vendor's share.

Each vendor is onboarded as a Route linked account with its own KYC held by Razorpay. A vendor that has not completed that onboarding cannot receive money, and the platform must block it from selling until it has — see section 6.

4.Flow 1 — Customer checkout and payment capture

Figure 1 — Checkout. The webhook is the source of truth; the browser callback is never trusted on its own.

4.1 Step by step

#StepWhat happens
1Cart validatedServer re-prices the cart from the database — never from what the client sent. Stock, MOQ, B2B tier eligibility, coupon validity and PIN-code serviceability are all re-checked at this moment.
2Payment order createdKrozenda's server calls Razorpay to create a payment order for the final total, with our internal order reference as the receipt. The amount is fixed server-side, in paise.
3Checkout openedThe buyer completes payment on Razorpay's interface. Card numbers, CVV and UPI PINs are entered there and never touch Krozenda's servers.
4Callback receivedThe client returns a payment id, order id and signature. We verify the signature, but treat this only as a hint that payment may have succeeded.
5Webhook receivedRazorpay calls our server directly with payment.captured. We verify the HMAC signature against our webhook secret. This is the event that confirms the order.
6Order confirmedThe order moves to Payment Verified, sub-orders are created, and vendors are notified. The buyer sees confirmation.
Why the webhook and not the callback. The browser callback can be lost — the app is killed, the network drops, the user closes the tab mid-redirect. It can also be forged by a determined attacker replaying a response. The server-to-server webhook is neither. We build so that a customer whose app crashed the instant after paying still gets a confirmed order, because the webhook arrives regardless.

5.Flow 2 — Order split and commission snapshot

A single cart can contain items from several sellers. At confirmation, the parent Order is split into one Sub-Order per seller, and each sub-order carries its own financial identity from that moment on.

Figure 2 — One payment, many sub-orders. Each sub-order is financially self-contained.

5.1 Commission is snapshotted, never recomputed

Commission is resolved at the moment the order is placed, using a most-specific-wins lookup, and the resulting figure is written onto the sub-order:

product-specific → seller-specific → category-specific → company-specific → default rate

Once written, that number never changes. If the client later raises the default commission from 8% to 10%, orders placed yesterday still settle at 8%. This matters commercially — it is what makes historical settlement reports reproducible and defensible in a vendor dispute — and it is why the commission figure lives on the sub-order record rather than being looked up from live configuration at settlement time.

6.Flow 3 — Vendor onboarding as a linked account

A vendor cannot receive a single rupee until Razorpay has onboarded and verified it. The sequence is:

  1. Vendor applies on Krozenda and uploads its documents — PAN, GST certificate, cancelled cheque, address proof, and category licences such as FSSAI.
  2. Krozenda's admin reviews them manually and approves or rejects with a reason. This is a human check against the documents supplied; it is not an automated verification against a government database.
  3. Krozenda creates a Route linked account for the approved vendor, submitting the vendor's legal name, business type, contact details, and bank account.
  4. Razorpay runs its own KYC on the linked account. This is independent of Krozenda's review and can take several working days. Razorpay may request further documents directly.
  5. The storefront activates only once the linked account reaches an active, transfer-eligible state.
Operational consequence to plan for. Razorpay's KYC on a linked account is not instant and is outside Krozenda's control. A vendor approved by your admin on Monday may not be able to receive money until the following week. The Seller Panel must show this state honestly — "approved by Krozenda, awaiting payment partner verification" — or your support team will field the complaints. The platform must also hard-block order assignment to any vendor whose linked account is not yet transfer-eligible, otherwise the money has nowhere to go at settlement.
Gap flagged: no penny-drop verification. Bank account verification by penny drop is outside the agreed scope. Without it, a vendor who mistypes an account number will have a linked account that looks valid but fails at transfer time, or — worse — succeeds into someone else's account. Razorpay's own linked-account KYC mitigates much of this, but not the typo case. We recommend the client either enables penny-drop verification as a scope addition, or accepts a documented manual check of the cancelled cheque against the entered account number during admin review. This needs a decision before go-live.

7.Flow 4 — Automated vendor settlement

This is the "auto payout" for vendors. It runs without human intervention on a per-sub-order basis, and it works by transferring the vendor's share early but held, then releasing it when the order is safely delivered.

Figure 3 — Route settlement. The vendor's share is ring-fenced at capture and released only when the order is safe.

7.1 Why hold, rather than transfer late

There are two ways to run Route: create the transfer immediately but hold it, or wait until delivery and create it then. We recommend transfer-on-hold at capture, for three reasons:

  • The money is ring-fenced from day one. The vendor's share is allocated against that specific payment, so a later dispute cannot leave it unfunded.
  • Reversal is clean. A cancellation or refund reverses a known transfer against a known payment, instead of requiring us to decide whether to create one at all.
  • The vendor can see it. "₹4,200 allocated, releasing on 14 March" is a far better Seller Panel experience than silence until settlement day, and it materially reduces settlement support tickets.

7.2 The release gate

A held transfer is released automatically when all of the following are true:

ConditionDetail
Status is DeliveredConfirmed by the logistics partner's delivery webhook, not by the vendor's own claim.
Return window elapsed[48] hours from delivery, matching the return-request window in the Terms & Conditions.
No open claimNo return, replacement or damage claim is pending on that sub-order.
No RTO in flightThe shipment has not been marked for return to origin.
Vendor account in good standingNot suspended, and no active fraud investigation or regulatory hold.

A scheduled job evaluates eligible sub-orders on the vendor's configured cycle — automatic, weekly or monthly. Where the cycle is automatic, release happens as soon as the gate opens. Where it is weekly or monthly, eligible transfers accumulate and release together on the cycle date, which suits vendors who prefer one consolidated credit and one reconciliation.

7.3 Settlement record

Every release writes an immutable settlement record carrying the full arithmetic, so that a vendor query years later can be answered from data rather than from reconstruction:

gross sale value − platform commission (snapshotted at order time) − payment gateway charge, where borne by the vendor − shipping charge, where borne by the vendor − penalties, RTO costs, and prior-cycle recoveries − tax deducted at source = net settled → transfer id · UTR · status · timestamp

A retail customer checks out a cart containing one item from Seller A and one from Seller B. Figures below use a [10%] commission on A and [8%] on B, and are illustrative.

Of the ₹10,200 collected, ₹8,680 is routed to the two sellers' linked accounts and ₹1,320 is retained by Krozenda — of which ₹920 is commission revenue, ₹200 is recovered shipping, and ₹200 is tax collected and deducted that Krozenda must deposit with the authorities, not keep. Confirm before build. The TCS and TDS rates and — more importantly — whether Krozenda or the seller is the deductor for each business model, depend on the seller-of-record decision still open in section 19. The arithmetic above assumes Krozenda deducts. If that changes, the split changes.

9.Flow 5 — Refunds and partial refunds

A refund is not a payout. It reverses the original payment back to the instrument the customer paid with — the same card, the same UPI handle — through the gateway's Refunds API. Money never leaves Krozenda's bank account to make a refund happen.

9.1 The two halves of a refund

Every refund on a multi-vendor order has two movements that must both succeed:

MovementWhat it does
Refund to buyerReturns the sub-order's value to the buyer's original payment instrument.
Transfer reversalClaws back the corresponding held or released transfer from the vendor's linked account, so the vendor is not paid for a sale that was undone.

These must be treated as one atomic business operation. Refunding the buyer without reversing the transfer means Krozenda has funded the refund out of its own money — the single most common and most expensive bug in marketplace settlement. The implementation records both legs against the sub-order and reconciles them nightly; any sub-order with one leg complete and the other not is raised as an exception the next morning.

9.2 Timing determines the mechanism

WhenWhat happens
Before dispatch — buyer cancelsTransfer is still on hold. It is reversed directly and the buyer is refunded in full including shipping. Cleanest case.
After delivery, claim upheldIf the transfer is still held, reverse and refund. If already released, a reversal is raised against the vendor's linked account; where its balance is insufficient, the amount is recovered from the vendor's next settlement cycle.
Partial — one item of severalOnly that sub-order's proportional value is refunded and only its transfer reversed. Sibling sub-orders are untouched.
Outside the gateway's refund windowThe original payment can no longer be reversed. Compensation is paid instead by RazorpayX Payout to the customer's bank account — one of the few legitimate customer-facing uses of Rail B. Requires finance approval.
What the buyer experiences. Refunds are initiated within [24 to 48] hours of approval, but the credit appears in [5 to 7] working days because the last leg belongs to the buyer's bank, not to us. Setting that expectation in the app at the moment the refund is approved — with the reference number visible — is the difference between a satisfied customer and a support ticket on day three.

10.Flow 6 — RTO and settlement reversal

A Return to Origin occurs when a shipment cannot be delivered — a wrong address, a customer unreachable after repeated attempts, or a refused delivery. RTO is not covered by the No-Return policy and has its own money path.

RTO Initiated → shipment in return transit → RTO Delivered (back to vendor) ↓ transfer reversed · inventory restored · buyer refunded per allocation rule
Cause of the RTOMoney outcome
Courier failure or vendor errorBuyer refunded in full including shipping. Vendor's transfer reversed. Return freight borne per the configured platform/vendor rule.
Buyer-attributable — bad address, unreachable, refusedBuyer refunded the product value; forward and return freight actually incurred may be deducted and is disclosed before deduction. Vendor's transfer reversed.

In both cases inventory is restored for Own Stock and Marketplace models, and the reversal is written to the settlement ledger against the original transfer id so the vendor's statement reconciles.

11.Automated payroll via RazorpayX Payouts

Everything to this point has been marketplace money on Rail A. Payroll is different: it is Krozenda paying its own staff out of its own funds, and it belongs on Rail B.

Figure 4 — Automated payroll. Krozenda's own balance, disbursed on a schedule to verified fund accounts.

11.1 What "automated payroll" means here

The platform holds a salary register — employee, designation, monthly gross, deductions, net payable, and a verified bank account or UPI handle. On the configured pay date a scheduled job reads the register, generates one payout instruction per employee, submits them, and tracks each to completion. Finance approves the run; the system executes it.

CapabilityBehaviour
Scheduled runFires on [the last working day of each month], or on demand for off-cycle payments.
Maker–checker approvalThe run is prepared by one user and released by another. No single person can move payroll money alone.
Balance pre-checkTotal run value is compared against available balance before submission; a short balance halts the run rather than paying some staff and not others.
Per-employee trackingEach payout carries its own status and UTR, visible in the admin panel.
Automatic retryA payout that fails for a transient reason is retried on the next cycle; one that fails for a bad account is flagged for correction and never retried blindly.
Audit trailWho prepared, who approved, what was sent, what was returned — retained for statutory audit.
Scope note. This is salary disbursement, not a full payroll system. Computing gross-to-net — PF, ESI, professional tax, TDS on salary under section 192, leave and attendance, Form 16 — is a separate body of work and is not in the current scope. What is described here takes an approved net-payable figure and moves the money reliably. If the client needs statutory payroll computation, that should be scoped separately or handled in dedicated payroll software that hands us the net figures.

12.The Payout API in detail

RazorpayX models a payout in three layers. Each layer is created once and reused, which keeps the recurring monthly run cheap and safe.

LayerCreatedHolds
ContactOnce per person or entityName, email, phone, type — employee, vendor, customer. The who.
Fund accountOnce per bank account or UPI handleAccount number and IFSC, or the VPA. Belongs to a contact. The where.
PayoutOnce per paymentAmount, mode, purpose, reference, narration. The what and when.
// Monthly salary run — one instruction per employee POST /v1/payouts X-Payout-Idempotency: "payroll-2026-03-emp-00417" // prevents double payment on retry { "account_number": "[Krozenda RazorpayX account]", "fund_account_id": "fa_xxxxxxxxxxxx", "amount": 4250000, // paise — ₹42,500.00 "currency": "INR", "mode": "IMPS", // IMPS | NEFT | RTGS | UPI "purpose": "salary", "queue_if_low_balance": true, // hold, don't fail, if balance is short "reference_id": "PAYROLL-2026-03-00417", "narration": "Krozenda Salary Mar 2026" }

12.1 Choosing the mode

ModeSpeedUse it for
IMPSImmediate, 24×7Salaries and anything time-sensitive. Per-transaction ceiling applies.
NEFTBatched, banking hoursBulk non-urgent transfers where cost matters more than speed.
RTGSReal-time, banking hoursHigh-value single transfers above the RTGS floor.
UPIImmediate, 24×7Small-value payouts to a VPA. Lower ceiling than IMPS.

12.2 Payout lifecycle

queued → pending → processing → processed ✓ money delivered, UTR issued ↘ reversed ✗ bank returned it — funds back in your balance ↘ failed ✗ never left — safe to correct and retry ↘ rejected ✗ validation or approval refused it ↘ cancelled — cancelled while still queued

The distinction between failed and reversed matters operationally. Failed means the money never left — correct the account details and retry. Reversed means it left, the beneficiary bank rejected it, and it came back; the employee has not been paid, the balance has been restored, and finance must be told. Both must be surfaced distinctly in the admin panel rather than collapsed into "error".

Idempotency is not optional. Every payout request must carry a unique idempotency key derived from the payroll period and the employee — never a random value, or the protection is lost. Without it, a network timeout on a request that actually succeeded will, on retry, pay the employee twice. Recovering a duplicate salary payment is a manual, relationship-damaging process. This is the single highest-risk detail in the payout implementation.

13.Payout automation and scheduling

The automated run executes in this order, and stops at the first failure rather than proceeding partially:

  1. Assemble. Read the salary register for the period; exclude leavers and unapproved rows.
  2. Validate. Confirm every included employee has an active fund account and a non-zero net payable.
  3. Total and check balance. Compare the run value against the available RazorpayX balance.
  4. Present for approval. Finance sees headcount, total value and exceptions, and approves or rejects the whole run.
  5. Submit. One payout per employee, each with its own idempotency key, submitted with controlled concurrency to stay inside rate limits.
  6. Track. Persist every payout id against its register row; update status from webhooks.
  7. Reconcile and report. Once all payouts are terminal, produce the run report: paid, failed, reversed, pending — with UTRs.

The same machinery serves off-cycle disbursements — a vendor incentive, a customer goodwill payment outside the refund window, an employee reimbursement — differing only in the purpose field and the approval threshold.

14.Webhooks and reconciliation

Webhooks are how the platform learns what actually happened. Every one is verified by HMAC signature against the webhook secret before it is trusted, and rejected outright if the signature does not match.

EventWhat the platform does
payment.capturedConfirm the order, create sub-orders, notify vendors.
payment.failedMark the attempt failed; leave the cart intact so the buyer can retry.
order.paidCross-check against our own order total; flag any mismatch.
refund.processedClose the refund, notify the buyer, complete the ledger entry.
refund.failedRaise an exception for finance — the buyer is still owed money.
transfer.processedMark the vendor's settlement complete and publish it to the Seller Panel.
transfer.failedHold the settlement, alert admin, block further releases to that vendor pending investigation.
payout.processedMark the salary or disbursement paid; record the UTR.
payout.reversedMark unpaid, restore the amount to the run's outstanding total, alert finance.
payout.failedFlag for correction; do not retry automatically without a human check.

14.1 Three rules the implementation follows

  • Verify before trusting. An unsigned or badly-signed webhook is discarded and logged as a security event.
  • Handle idempotently. Razorpay may deliver the same event more than once. Every handler is safe to run repeatedly — a second payment.captured for an order already confirmed changes nothing.
  • Never assume delivery. Webhooks can be missed entirely. A nightly reconciliation job independently fetches the day's payments, refunds, transfers and payouts from Razorpay and compares them against our records, correcting drift and reporting anything it cannot reconcile.
Why the nightly job earns its keep. Webhook delivery is reliable but not guaranteed — an outage on our side during a delivery window loses events permanently. Without independent reconciliation, a missed transfer.processed means a vendor is paid but the Seller Panel says otherwise, and the discrepancy is found by the vendor rather than by us. Reconciliation converts an unbounded class of silent errors into a bounded morning report.

16.Security and key management

  • No card data on our servers. Card numbers, CVV, UPI PINs and net-banking credentials are captured on Razorpay's PCI-DSS compliant interface. Krozenda stores only the payment reference, amount and status.
  • Amounts are server-authoritative. Every payable amount is computed on the server from the database. A price or total submitted by the client is never trusted.
  • Signature verification everywhere. Both the checkout callback and every webhook are HMAC-verified before any state changes.
  • Key segregation. Payment gateway keys and RazorpayX payout keys are separate credentials with separate blast radii, held in environment configuration, never in source control, and rotated on a schedule. Test and live keys are never present in the same environment.
  • Payout authorisation is privileged. Initiating or approving a payout is a distinct permission held by a small number of finance roles, separate from general admin, and every action is written to the immutable audit trail.
  • Maker–checker on money out. No single account can both prepare and release a payout run.
  • Rate limiting and replay protection on all payment endpoints, with webhook timestamps checked to reject replayed deliveries.

17.Tax treatment — TCS and TDS

As a marketplace operator, Krozenda has collection and deduction obligations on vendor sales:

ObligationTreatment
TCS — GST s.52Collected on the net taxable value of vendor supplies, deducted before settlement, deposited and reported in GSTR-8. The vendor claims credit in its own returns.
TDS — IT s.194-ODeducted on the gross sale value of vendor supplies, deposited, and reflected in the vendor's Form 26AS.
Own Stock salesNo TCS or TDS — Krozenda is the seller, not the facilitator. Ordinary GST output liability applies.
Salary payoutsTDS under s.192 is a payroll computation, outside this scope. Payouts move the approved net figure.

Both deductions appear as itemised lines on every settlement statement, and the platform generates period-wise reports for filing.

Summary of the compliance position. Vendor sale proceeds move on Razorpay Route and are never custodied by Krozenda. Krozenda's own funds — commission, own-stock revenue — settle to Krozenda's account and are disbursed through RazorpayX Payouts for salaries and platform expenses. Keeping these two rails separate is what allows Krozenda to operate a multi-vendor marketplace without holding a Payment Aggregator licence.
